Hashing:
- Designed to be one-way and produces fixed-length digests. This helps to verify data or compare the values without storing the original, such as passwords.
Encryption:
- Secret are used to turn texts into ciphertext that can look like random data. This helps to keep data confidential while only allowing the authorized person to recover it, such as with emails, symmetrical encryption, and asymmetrical encryption.
Encoding:
- Things like base64 are used to just change how data is represented or transmitted. These are very easy to decode since no secret is needed.

Passwords:
- Passwords are hashed so that the data you enter that gets hashed can match the result in a stored value. A salt is a way to make identical passwords produce different stored hashes as well as prevent attackers from using lookup tables effectively.
- Strong passwords are used since they require many more guesses than short and predictable passwords. Reusing passwords can also cause many problems, such as one breached account leading to many breached accounts due to one shared password.
- MFA is multifactor authentication, which required multiple forms (typically two) forms of authentication before logging into an account. This can prevent some hackers from entering into your account since they need more than just a password to get it. They can still be breached though, so certain passkeys and hardware security keys work best due to them being able to resist phishing attacks.
